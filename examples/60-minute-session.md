## 00–20 min · Progressive warm-up
Raise temperature, move comfortably, activate lightly, then rehearse today’s tasks.
Proposed routine pending coach and physiotherapist review. Not a validated BJJ injury-prevention protocol. Finish warm and coordinated, without fatigue. Completion is not clearance to roll.

### 00–05 min · Raise temperature
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- March, shuffle, and stance movement: Build gradually at a conversational pace. Start with brisk marching; progress to gentle lateral shuffles and stance movement.
  Easier option: Stay with easy walking or marching.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/
### 05–10 min · Dynamic mobility
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Ankle rocks: 5–8 controlled repetitions per side. Keep heels down; move through comfortable range.
  Easier option: Use a smaller range near a wall.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/foot-and-ankle-conditioning-program/
- Shallow squats and hip hinges: 5–8 easy repetitions of each. Keep feet stable and knees tracking with toes; avoid planted-foot twisting.
  Easier option: Use a shallow supported squat.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/
- Gentle adductor rock-backs: 5–8 controlled repetitions per side. From hands and knees, extend one leg sideways and gently move hips back. Do not force a wide position.
  Easier option: Reduce the leg angle and movement range.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/
- All-fours thoracic rotations: 5–8 gentle repetitions per side. Rotate comfortably through the upper back without forcing the neck.
  Easier option: Keep both hands down and use a smaller turn.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/spine-conditioning-program/
- Wrist motion, hand opening, and arm circles: 5 slow hand cycles and comfortable wrist/arm movements. Open and close hands gently; keep circles comfortable.
  Easier option: Use unloaded small movements; skip irritated fingers.
  Movement reference: https://www.nth.nhs.uk/resources/hand-therapy-tendon-gliding-exercises/
### 10–14 min · Light activation
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Glute bridges: One easy set of 8. Lift hips with control; support through shoulders and feet, never the head.
  Easier option: Use a smaller lift.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/
- Scapular push-ups from hands and knees: One easy set of 6–8. Keep elbows straight and move shoulder blades without shrugging.
  Easier option: Try a comfortable wall slide.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/
- Bird dogs: 4 slow repetitions per side. Keep pelvis steady; reach without twisting or arching.
  Easier option: Move one arm or one leg at a time.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/spine-conditioning-program/
- Single-leg balance: 20 seconds per side. Stand near support and keep the foot stable.
  Easier option: Use light fingertip support.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/foot-and-ankle-conditioning-program/
### 14–20 min · Grappling-specific ramp
Divide this phase into three easy parts: familiar ground movement; cooperative rehearsal of pin escapes, guard passing; then a simple positional task with gradually increasing resistance and no submission finishes. Skip excluded or unfamiliar movements. Put bands away before partner work.
- Familiar ground transitions: First third of the grappling-specific ramp. Easy shrimping, bridges supported through shoulders rather than head, and familiar technical stand-ups. Skip unfamiliar movements.
  Easier option: Cooperatively rehearse a comfortable start with little resistance.

## 20–30 min · Find your frames
Rounds: 2 × 5-minute rounds
- Round 1: 20–25 min (5 min)
- Round 2: 25–30 min (5 min)
Start: You begin on bottom in side control. Start with light chest contact and your elbows close to your body.
Your goal: Recover guard or come to a seated position.
Partner goal: Maintain controlled chest contact.
Reset: Reset on guard recovery or sitting up. Otherwise continue until the round ends.
Agree on the start and resistance before the round. Reset after each success and continue until the round ends. Use the chosen role for the round; if trading roles, switch at the next round. No separate rest intervals are added between rounds; take needed rest within the allotted time or use the labeled buffer. Warm-up and wrap-up use their own phase timings.

- No submissions. Stay within the starting position’s task.
- Use controlled resistance; reset rather than force a trapped joint.
- Agree on resistance before starting. Stop on any tap, pain, or request.
- No explosive bridging, neck cranks, slams, or jumping movements.
- Practice cue: Rehearse a forearm frame at the shoulder and a hip-side frame without resistance.

## 40–45 min · Escape the arm isolation
Rounds: 1 × 5-minute round
- Round 1: 40–45 min (5 min)

Your goal: Prevent arm isolation and recover guard or turtle.
Partner goal: Maintain the pin and isolate an arm without applying a lock.
Reset: Reset after escape; release arm control immediately if it strains the shoulder.

Partner goal: Remove your underhooks and recover a knee shield.
Reset: Reset after a pass or knee-shield recovery.

### 53–56 min · Comfortable mobility
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.

### 56–58 min · Relaxed breathing and body check
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Relaxed breathing and body check: Sit or lie comfortably; breathe normally. Let the exhale lengthen only if comfortable. No breath holds or hyperventilation. Notice new pain, swelling, or loss of function; do not stretch those symptoms away.
  Easier option: Sit supported and breathe normally.

### 58–60 min · Optional reflection
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Optional reflection: Note games, time, RPE, and one observation. What worked? What should change next time? Note symptoms that should change your next plan. This is a prompt, not a saved journal or diagnosis.
  Easier option: Skip the journal and rest quietly.
Afterward, rehydrate, eat according to normal needs, change damp training clothes, and follow gym hygiene practices. New or persistent symptoms should change subsequent training and prompt appropriate assessment.
