### 00–05 min · Raise temperature
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- March, shuffle, and stance movement: Build gradually at a conversational pace. Start with brisk marching; progress to gentle lateral shuffles and stance movement.
  Easier option: Stay with easy walking or marching.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/
### 05–10 min · Dynamic mobility
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Ankle rocks: 5–8 controlled repetitions per side. Keep heels down; move through comfortable range.
  Easier option: Use a smaller range near a wall.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/foot-and-ankle-conditioning-program/
- Shallow squats and hip hinges: 5–8 easy repetitions of each. Keep feet stable and knees tracking with toes; avoid planted-foot twisting.
  Easier option: Use a shallow supported squat.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/
- Gentle adductor rock-backs: 5–8 controlled repetitions per side. From hands and knees, extend one leg sideways and gently move hips back. Do not force a wide position.
  Easier option: Reduce the leg angle and movement range.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/
- All-fours thoracic rotations: 5–8 gentle repetitions per side. Rotate comfortably through the upper back without forcing the neck.
  Easier option: Keep both hands down and use a smaller turn.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/spine-conditioning-program/
- Wrist motion, hand opening, and arm circles: 5 slow hand cycles and comfortable wrist/arm movements. Open and close hands gently; keep circles comfortable.
  Easier option: Use unloaded small movements; skip irritated fingers.
  Movement reference: https://www.nth.nhs.uk/resources/hand-therapy-tendon-gliding-exercises/
### 10–14 min · Light activation
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Glute bridges: One easy set of 8. Lift hips with control; support through shoulders and feet, never the head.
  Easier option: Use a smaller lift.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/
- Scapular push-ups from hands and knees: One easy set of 6–8. Keep elbows straight and move shoulder blades without shrugging.
  Easier option: Try a comfortable wall slide.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/
- Bird dogs: 4 slow repetitions per side. Keep pelvis steady; reach without twisting or arching.
  Easier option: Move one arm or one leg at a time.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/spine-conditioning-program/
- Single-leg balance: 20 seconds per side. Stand near support and keep the foot stable.
  Easier option: Use light fingertip support.
  Movement reference: https://www.orthoinfo.org/recovery/foot-and-ankle-conditioning-program/
### 14–20 min · Grappling-specific ramp
Divide this phase into three easy parts: familiar stance movement and entries, with previously learned, coached breakfalls only; cooperative rehearsal of takedowns; then a simple positional task with gradually increasing resistance and no submission finishes. Skip excluded or unfamiliar movements. Put bands away before partner work.

## 20–30 min · Find the knee
Rounds: 2 × 5-minute rounds
- Round 1: 20–25 min (5 min)
- Round 2: 25–30 min (5 min)
Start: Both start standing without grips; you are the attacker. Start at mutually agreed light-touch distance without grips.
Your goal: Make repeated light touches to either knee without grabbing or driving through.
Partner goal: Use distance, movement, and hands to deny knee touches.
Reset: Continuous light-touch task; return to comfortable distance after each touch and continue. No takedown score.
Agree on the start and resistance before the round. Reset after each success and continue until the round ends. Use the chosen role for the round; if trading roles, switch at the next round. No separate rest intervals are added between rounds; take needed rest within the allotted time or use the labeled buffer. Warm-up and wrap-up use their own phase timings.

- No submissions, jumping entries, slams, sacrifice throws, or forced joint twisting.
- Use only familiar, coached movements. No completed takedowns or lifts. Stay standing; stop and reset if balance is lost.
- Agree on resistance and boundaries. Use a clear, suitable matted area away from other pairs. Stop on any tap, pain, or request.
- Release a trapped leg rather than twist a planted knee. No neck pulling. Keep movement controlled; do not force a fall.
- Practice cue: Rehearse stance movement and a small level change without diving forward.
Source inspiration: https://slimemoldgrappling.com/games/take_downs/knee_tag/index.html

## 30–40 min · Keep the single-leg connection
Rounds: 2 × 5-minute rounds
- Round 1: 30–35 min (5 min)
- Round 2: 35–40 min (5 min)
Start: You begin standing as attacker with one of your partner’s legs held between your legs. Begin with a light established single-leg connection and stable balance.
Your goal: Maintain the single-leg connection while moving slowly together.
Partner goal: Stay balanced on the free foot and free the captured leg.
Reset: Reset when the partner frees the leg, or immediately when either player loses balance. Your connection task is continuous.

## 40–45 min · Connect the body lock
Rounds: 1 × 5-minute round
- Round 1: 40–45 min (5 min)
Start: Both start standing with reciprocal underhooks; you are working to connect your hands. Start with reciprocal underhooks and equal inside position.
Your goal: Get inside or behind the elbows and join your hands behind the partner’s back.
Partner goal: Deny your hand connection and seek their own body lock.
Reset: Reset immediately when either player joins hands behind the back. No squeeze, lift, or throw after the connection.
Agree on the start and resistance before the round. Reset after each success and continue until the round ends. Use the chosen role for the round; if trading roles, switch at the next round. ## 45–50 min · Single-leg off-balance
Rounds: 1 × 5-minute round
- Round 1: 45–50 min (5 min)
Start: You begin as attacker in a captured single leg, with both partners balanced and ready. Begin with an established single-leg connection and gentle resistance.
Your goal: Maintain connection and guide the partner onto hands or hips with a slow, controlled off-balance.
Partner goal: Maintain balance and free the captured leg.
Reset: Reset after controlled contact of hands or hips with the mat, or when the leg is freed. Release and reset if the descent cannot be controlled.
Agree on the start and resistance before the round. Reset after each success and continue until the round ends. Use the chosen role for the round; if trading roles, switch at the next round. No separate rest intervals are added between rounds; take needed rest within the allotted time or use the labeled buffer. Warm-up and wrap-up use their own phase timings.

- No submissions, jumping entries, slams, sacrifice throws, or forced joint twisting.
- Use only familiar, coached movements. Only a slow, controlled descent to hands or hips is allowed; no lifts or airborne finishes.
- Agree on resistance and boundaries. Use a clear, suitable matted area away from other pairs. Stop on any tap, pain, or request.
- Release a trapped leg rather than twist a planted knee. No neck pulling. Both partners must already know how to receive the movement safely; otherwise use the connection-only game.
- Practice cue: Cooperatively rehearse a familiar, coached off-balance and safe receiving movement first.
Source inspiration: https://slimemoldgrappling.com/games/take_downs/single_leg/connection_and_destabilization/index.html

### 58–60 min · Optional reflection
Use the phase as an easy time window, not a repetition target. Leave time for rest and transitions; do not rush to complete every movement.
- Optional reflection: Note games, time, RPE, and one observation. What worked? What should change next time? Note symptoms that should change your next plan. This is a prompt, not a saved journal or diagnosis.
  Easier option: Skip the journal and rest quietly.
Afterward, rehydrate, eat according to normal needs, change damp training clothes, and follow gym hygiene practices. New or persistent symptoms should change subsequent training and prompt appropriate assessment.
